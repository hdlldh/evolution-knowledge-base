## 1. Missing Items (present in the text but absent or thin in the extraction)

- **The visceral second-person "Imagine this" opening.** The Introduction opens with an extended
  second-person scenario (you're 23, one shot, the world watching; success = a respectful nod at most,
  failure = defining you forever; you're exhausted after two hours and a month of duty; an opponent
  smiles and taunts you; and you're unprepared because your boss thinks penalties can't be practised —
  "Dread. Anxiety. Fear. Welcome to the penalty shootout."). This is the book's rhetorical entry point and
  a compact inventory of the *sources* of shootout pressure (stakes asymmetry, fatigue, provocation, lack
  of preparation). The extraction conveys pieces of this but never captures the opening as a unit or the
  fatigue element. Added a card/example.

- **Fatigue as a pressure source.** The "you're exhausted… two hours of hard work… a whole month on
  duty" point is a distinct, concrete stressor the extraction omitted from its pressure model. Worth a
  line.

- **The France manager's specific belief.** The text twice attributes France's unpreparedness to a
  manager who "stated numerous times his belief that it is impossible to practise penalties." The
  extraction captures the fact but this is a specific, named-later fallacy the book will attack; keep it
  prominent (it pairs with Wenger's counter-practice). Adequately represented but worth sharpening.

- **Harry Kane and Roberto Baggio (Wenger's examples).** Wenger opens with Kane missing the target
  altogether vs. France (2022) as evidence of pressure, and closes with Baggio still thinking about his
  1994 miss ("it's worth being prepared"). The extraction mentions both only inside the Wenger case;
  neither is broken out. Minor, but Baggio/Kane are two of the most famous misses in the sport and are
  reusable — noted.

- **Wenger's FIFA/structural point.** Wenger notes the World Cup expanding 32→48 teams (one more knockout
  stage) and the 2025 Club World Cup — i.e., penalties will become *more* frequent, reinforcing the book's
  base-rate argument from the game's governance side. The extraction omits this; it strengthens Claim/
  Card 04. Added.

## 2. Corrections Needed (statements that distort the text)

- **"France's manager" unnamed.** The extraction correctly does not name Deschamps (the text doesn't name
  him in these pages) — good; it says "the France manager." Confirmed, no invention. No change.

- **Beckham miss magnitude.** The extraction says "1–2 m over the bar"; the text says "between one and two
  metres over the crossbar." Match. Confirmed.

- **Viewership figures.** "1.5 billion… 29 million in France (record)" — matches the text (with endnote
  markers). Confirmed.

- **Wenger's record.** "15 shootouts… won the first two, then lost four in a row, but won eight of the
  last nine" — matches. Confirmed. (Note: 2 + 4 + 9 = 15; internally consistent.)

## 3. Overgeneralizations (author hedges flattened)

- **"Being a superstar is a liability."** The extraction correctly labels this "Interpretive /
  Observational" and flags that it's contested by Berg and "not yet quantified in the Introduction." Good
  — the hedge is preserved. (The book presumably quantifies it later; the Introduction only asserts a
  pattern.)

- **The natural-laboratory advantages.** Stated by Jordet as benefits; the extraction presents them as a
  framework while flagging the observational-not-experimental limitation. Adequate.

## 4. Important Nuance Lost

- **The women's-game data gap is also a substantive finding, not just a caveat.** The base-rate stats
  show women's tournaments go to penalties at different rates (WC 11% vs. men's 20%; but Copa América
  Femenina 30%, equal to the men's). The extraction lists the numbers and flags the male skew, but the
  *asymmetry itself* (women's WC far lower, Copa equal) is an interesting datum the extraction treats only
  as a limitation. Minor; retained in Card 04 and Tension #2.

- **"My interest rapidly became a mission" / the decade-long full-circle with Berg.** The extraction
  captures the full-circle Legia Warsaw ending inside the Beckham/Berg case. Good — this narrative
  bookend (crushed by Berg on air → later celebrating in Berg's dressing room, Berg's team winning on
  penalties) is a strong storytelling beat and is preserved. Adequate.

## 5. Additional Cases Worth Capturing

  Wenger; individually they don't need full case blocks in a framing chapter, but they belong in the
  quotables/examples as reusable references. Folded into the Wenger material and a quotable.

## 7. Potential Disagreements / Counterpoints the Chapter Doesn't Raise

- **The Berg position is the built-in counterpoint** and the extraction foregrounds it (Tension #1). Good.
- **Selection/survivorship in the cases.** The Introduction's vivid cases (Beckham, Mbappé) are chosen
  precisely because they are dramatic; a skeptic would note that a few famous misses don't establish that
  stars miss *more* on average. The extraction's Claim #2 strength rating ("Moderate… not yet quantified")
  already implies this. Adequate.
