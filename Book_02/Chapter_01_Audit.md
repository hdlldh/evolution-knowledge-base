## 1. Missing Items (present in the text but absent or thin in the extraction)

- **The early-shootout / rushing history (1980 Kempes & Rix; 1984 Tottenham–Anderlecht; Gary Stevens;
  Enzo Scifo).** The chapter opens with a substantial historical passage on how *raw and rushed* the first
  shootouts were — Mario Kempes (1978 World Cup winner and superstar) missing in the 1980 Cup Winners' Cup
  final (the first living legend "stripped of dignity"); Graham Rix's miss handing Arsenal the first
  European-final loss on penalties; and the 1984 UEFA Cup final where nine of ten takers reacted "as if
  [the whistle] were the crack of a starting pistol," Gary Stevens kicking *simultaneously* with the
  whistle, and 18-year-old Enzo Scifo's composed one-second pause standing out. The extraction jumps
  straight to Stielike/Six and omits this opening, which both (a) introduces the "speeding up" theme early
  and (b) gives the Kempes "superstar stripped of dignity" beat. Added a case.

- **The individual-vs-team choking distinction.** The chapter carefully argues that choking is easy to
  see in *individual* sports (van de Velde, Novotná, Cate Campbell) but ambiguous in *team* sports (AC
  Milan 2005, Bayern 1999, Brazil 2014 might be opponent excellence, not choking) — which is precisely why
  the penalty, where football "approaches the condition of an individual sport," is the clean case. The
  extraction lists the individual exemplars in §15 but never states this individual-vs-team argument,
  which is the logical bridge to "why penalties are the ideal choking laboratory." Added.

- **The specific psychophysiological "can help / can backfire" split.** The extraction's Card 02 lists the
  cascade but compresses the two-sided consequence. The text is explicit that narrowed attention *helps*
  (less distractible) while the *same* processes backfire (insufficient situational awareness, flawed
  judgment, more cognitive errors, increased risk-taking, worse fine-motor control). Adequately captured
  in Card 02 and §13, but worth confirming the double-edged framing is preserved. No change needed.

- **"You cannot not communicate" as a named principle.** The extraction includes the phrase in the
  avoidance-gaze card, but it is a load-bearing idea (Watzlawick's axiom) that sets up Chapter 4's "team
  game" theme. Adequately represented; noting for the cross-chapter thread.

## 2. Corrections Needed (statements that distort the text)

- **Rashford pause-window dating.** The extraction's Card 04 and case say the 11-second pause is the
  longest "in all 718 elite kicks coded (1976–today)." The text says "all the 718 penalty kicks taken in
  the World Cup, Euros and Champions League for men between 1976 and today." Note the corpus is described
  two ways in the chapter: "1970–2023" (the descriptive shame-behaviour analysis, p. 21) and "1976 and
  today" (the pause analysis, p. 30). Both are in the text; the extraction correctly uses 1970–2023 for
  the shame corpus and should use 1976 for the pause claim. Confirmed the extraction already says "1976"
  in Card 04 — good. No change, but flagging the two date ranges so they aren't "corrected" into one.

- **Quiet-eye timing figures.** "68% → 57%" free-throw conversion — matches the text. Confirmed.

- **Negative/positive/neutral percentages.** 63% / 89% / 72% — match. In-game vs. shootout: French Cup
  73.1% shootout (252 shootouts/2504 kicks) vs. 76% Ligue 1; ~4% higher in-game — all match. Confirmed.

- **Avoidance percentages.** 44% (must-score) / 14% (must-win) / 30% (neutral); 57% English; 72% vs. 74%
  overall; 87% vs. 70% forwards — all match the text. Confirmed.

## 3. Overgeneralizations (author hedges flattened)

- **"Overthinking explains Rashford/Rapinoe."** The extraction is careful here — it repeatedly labels the
  attributions "candidate cases" / "speculative," mirroring Jordet's own hedge ("inevitably speculative,"
  "might still just be coincidences"). Good.

- **Avoidance gaze impact.** The extraction correctly foregrounds the "72% vs. 74%… statistically
  meaningless" overall null and the strategic-vs-reactive split, rather than overclaiming that avoidance
  gaze causes misses. Good; the Bundesliga finer-coding caveat is included.

- **Negative-shot effect.** The extraction includes both the replication and the non-replication and
  Jordet's elite-sample caveat. Good — not flattened.

## 4. Important Nuance Lost

- **The 53% shame-behaviour corpus is 1970–2023; the pause corpus is 1976–today.** These are two
  different analyses on overlapping-but-distinct spans. The extraction keeps them straight; preserve that
  (see §2). No fix beyond vigilance.

- **"Determination" as the most common positive emotion (8 of 10).** The extraction includes this. Good —
  it's the seed of Chapter 2's coping (positive emotions coexist with anxiety), so worth keeping visible.

- **The distraction *vs.* self-focus dichotomy.** The chapter presents two *competing* families of
  choking theory — overthinking/self-focus (attention inward) and distraction (attention pulled away from
  the target, via quiet eye). The extraction captures both but could state more sharply that they are
  near-opposite mechanisms ("an almost opposite view"). Minor; the concepts and cards preserve both.

## 7. Potential Disagreements / Counterpoints the Chapter Doesn't Raise

  extraction's Tension #1 carries this. Good.

  (Unique Ideas: sudden orthodoxies) but could foreground as a limitation. Adequate.
