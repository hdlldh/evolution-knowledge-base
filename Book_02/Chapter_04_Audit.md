Audited against `Chapter_04_Extraction.md` by re-reading the original chapter text (PDF pp. 128–163), not
the extraction. The extraction captures the six-part collective toolkit, the HRO pivot, the belonging thesis
and the give-and-take model well. The misses are concentrated in (a) a genuinely novel, reusable

---

## Missing Items

1. **Neuro 11 / Dr Niklas Häusler (pp.153–154) — a total omission.** Liverpool's four-shootout winning
   streak (since beating Leicester, Dec 2021) is attributed partly to work with the German neuroscience
   company **Neuro 11**, which uses **objective brainwave feedback during on-pitch training** to help
   players build pre-performance routines and reach "optimal mental states under pressure." Founder/CEO
   **Dr Niklas Häusler**: their work produced a "team-wide awareness to assess even psychologically very
   complex situations… more correctly and help others more efficiently"; and of the Alisson handoff, "the
   team has developed a brilliant dynamic of helping and understanding each other when it matters the most."
   This is novel (neurofeedback for penalty prep), reusable, and it pairs with the chapter's own "this may
   be coincidence" hedge on the streak — a correlational caveat the extraction should carry. It also opens a
   real BCI/closed-loop AI bridge.

2. **Maren Mjelde & Jess Carter (pp.144–145) — a distinct tailored-emotional-support case.** Mjelde had
   pre-assigned "protectors" ("there are supposed to be two protectors… just like we have two penalty
   takers"). At the spot, her friend Jess Carter kept people away *and* used light humour ("This is dragging
   out, Maren, is there anything you want to talk about?"… "Wanna grab a coffee next week?"), removing grass
   from her face — "what Jess did will take down the seriousness of it all, for just a second or two… Otherwise,
   one can just lock up completely." This illustrates the *emotional* (not just functional) support end and
   the "protector" role structure — distinct from Mušović (keeper handoff) and Henderson (functional screening).

3. **Ødegaard's "organic emergence" quote on the decoy (pp.148–149).** "We haven't spoken about this
   really, it has just happened automatically. First we agree who will take the penalty. Then it is up to
   him what he needs…" — worth one line, as it frames the decoy as player-led and needs-based, not a
   top-down set play.

4. **Colour quotes:** Harry Catterick's "these penalties to decide a match are like a circus" (1970); the
   premature Kleff celebration. Non-essential.

## Potential Disagreements to Track Later

- **HRO / "don't chase positivity" vs. sports-psychology orthodoxy.** Jordet's rejection of "think positive"
  in favour of error-normalisation collides with the confidence/positive-self-talk canon of mainstream sport
  psychology — a cross-book tension for the profile's §14/§16.
- **Neurofeedback efficacy.** Neuro 11's brainwave-feedback claims sit in a contested area (neurofeedback's
  evidence base is mixed); a skeptical reader would double down on the "may be coincidence" caveat.
- **Give-and-take** (already tracked in §11) — humane long-run vs. per-kick EV.
