# Pressure: Lessons From the Psychology of the Penalty Shoot-Out — Chapter 6: Managing the pressure
**Author:** Geir Jordet
**Type:** Audit

**Date:** 2026-07-24

Audited against `Chapter_06_Extraction.md` by re-reading the original chapter text (PDF pp. 182–207), not
the extraction. The extraction captures the Scaloni/Deschamps contrast, the shortest-time-wins finding, the
one-voice principle, the case against volunteering, the props/goalkeeper findings and the psychology-
revolution close well. The misses are concentrated in (a) three strong Scaloni-related quotes/labels that
deepen the people-first thesis, (b) the physiological-vs-psychological asymmetry framing, and (c) the
Deschamps "planned shooters" quote.

---

## Missing Items

1. **Scaloni's post-Saudi-Arabia car quote (p.183) — a strong, thesis-anchoring quote, omitted.** After the
   opening-match upset loss to Saudi Arabia, Scaloni was filmed saying: "We have to know that we can lose…
   it shapes our playing and it shapes our learning… If after receiving this blow, we recover… there will be
   no stopping us." This is the leadership analogue of Ch.4's "missing is natural" — *accepting the
   possibility of loss as the precondition for resilience* — and it belongs in §12.

2. **Scaloni's "peaceful leader" / "feminine leader" labels + Di María's "our father" (pp.182–183).**
   Leadership experts in Argentina call Scaloni "the peaceful leader," others a "feminine" leader — "labels
   rarely applied to football managers"; Di María: "He's our father, the captain of this ship… very
   affectionate." These sharpen the people-first concept and the vulnerability theme, and are reusable.

3. **The physiological-vs-psychological asymmetry (p.190) — a chapter-framing observation, omitted.** From
   the raw video, "nobody in a World Cup penalty shootout in the 21st century goes short of water" — towels,
   supplements, massages, herds of doctors/therapists; "elite football really does seem to have the
   physiological support thing nailed down. Regarding psychological support… there may be room for
   improvement." This one line frames the whole book's argument (and the psychology revolution): the
   resources exist for the body, not the mind.

4. **Deschamps's "all the planned shooters were no longer on the field" (p.198).** His own post-final
   explanation directly supports the "plan the end" challenge (you can't fully pre-list the 120-minute-plus
   lineup) — a good supporting quote the extraction references only in paraphrase.

5. **Minor colour:** Maradona's dismissal of Scaloni's appointment ("I don't want him… he couldn't even
   control traffic"); Scaloni's humility ("I am a coach of a group of players and nothing more"). Optional.
