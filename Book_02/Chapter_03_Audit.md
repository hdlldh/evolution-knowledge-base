# Pressure: Lessons From the Psychology of the Penalty Shoot-Out — Chapter 3: Exploiting the pressure
**Author:** Geir Jordet
**Type:** Audit
**Source:** sources/Pressure.Lessons.From.the.Psychology.of.the.Penalty.Shoot.Out.pdf, PDF pages 92–127 (book chapter 3)
**Date:** 2026-07-24

Audited against `Chapter_03_Extraction.md` by re-reading the original chapter text (PDF pp. 92–127), not
the extraction. The extraction captures the big cases, the taxonomy, and the honest anecdote-vs-data twist
well. The misses are concentrated in (a) two *null-result* tactics that actually strengthen the chapter's
thesis, (b) one under-hedged statistic, (c) a reusable player-list example, and (d) a rehearsal quote that
proves mind games are deliberate.

---

## Missing Items

1. **The penalty-spot-confrontation null result (p.106).** The extraction lists "penalty-spot
   confrontation" in the §4 taxonomy but never states the *finding*: Jordet's data shows keepers who show
   up at the spot (van Breukelen pointing to his eye, 1988; Ricardo vs. Beckham, 2004) do **not** make
   takers miss more than keepers who stay back — for Alves/Martínez it merely "creates a platform" for
   later, more effective acts. This is a third non-significant tactic and belongs in §5 and §9's "the
   famous tactics have the weakest data" argument.

2. **"Gardening" the penalty spot (p.105).** Omitted as a concrete example. Defenders scuff the spot with
   their boots — Argentina before Mbappé's 2022-final kicks; Arsenal (Ramsdale & Gabriel), Chelsea
   (Rüdiger), Liverpool (van Dijk), Man Utd (de Gea), Wolves (José Sá). Functional (harder to strike a
   scuffed spot) *and* psychological ("the defender has literally stepped on the attacker's turf"). A
   reusable, currently-topical example that should be a case.

3. **Martínez's rehearsal quote (p.98).** "I had practised it with my psychologist: if I manage to save one
   I make life impossible for the next one." This is materially important — it proves the mind games are
   *deliberately trained*, not improvised temperament. Should be in §12 and the Martínez case.

4. **Mind games "evolved in two directions" (p.124).** The chapter's framing — simultaneously *more
   intense/volatile/brutal* and *more subtle/complex/manipulative* — is a clean organising statement the
   extraction implies but never states. Minor but worth one line.

5. **Minor colour:** van Breukelen pointing to his own eye (1988); Reker's "four bins with data from
   1500–2000 players"; the specific two-water-bottle detail already captured. Non-essential.

## Corrections Needed

- **§5 baseline gloss.** The extraction compares Alves's 61% to "a ~75–78% norm." The book's shootout-
  specific baseline is lower than the in-game ~78–82% (Ch.2 gave middle 77.9%, sides ~71%); "absurdly low"
  is the author's phrase but the comparison norm is loose. Not an error, but flag the baseline is
  approximate/shootout-context rather than the in-game average.
- No factual misrepresentations found. Spot-checked figures (Alves 27/69 → 61%; Furley 322 shots, 10%;
  proliferation <10%→50–60%; forced wait >8s → 44%; van der Sar 8.5s vs 0.7s; off-kilter 6–8 cm → 60–64%;
  reputation ~6 cm / 2.36 in; VAR 62→114s, 71% vs 81%; icing ~10–14%) all match the text.

## Overgeneralizations

- **Reputation/market-value effect framed as robust (§2, §3, §9, §19).** The extraction rates it
  "Strong-ish." But the chapter says the keeper-market-value result was the *only* significant result in a
  study testing market value for both takers and keepers — and it "surprised the researchers." Being the
  lone significant finding among several tests is a classic multiple-comparisons red flag; it could be a
  false positive. The height-perception studies are separate and firmer, but the market-value leg should be
  hedged, not bundled into "robust."

## Important Nuance Lost

- The multiple-comparisons caveat above is the biggest one.
- The penalty-spot-confrontation null result (above) — its absence makes the "distraction is equivocal"
  thesis look less supported than the chapter actually makes it.
- That mind games are *rehearsed with a psychologist* (Martínez) reframes them from spontaneous personality
  to trainable tactic — a nuance the extraction currently loses.

## Additional Cases and Examples

```
Case Title: Gardening the penalty spot
People / Organization: Argentina (2022 WC final vs. Mbappé); Ramsdale & Gabriel (Arsenal), Rüdiger
(Chelsea), van Dijk (Liverpool), de Gea (Man Utd), José Sá (Wolves)
Context: In the chaos after a penalty award or VAR review, defenders scuff the penalty spot with their boots.
What Happened: The spot is roughed up before the taker can place the ball.
Outcome: Functional (harder to strike cleanly from a scuffed spot) and psychological (the defender has
"stepped on the attacker's turf").
Concept Illustrated: Physical/territorial disruption; low-cost, deniable gamesmanship.
Why This Case Is Useful: Topical, common at elite level, and a clean functional+psychological hybrid.
Potential for Reuse: Medium
```

```
Case Title: Penalty-spot confrontation — the tactic the data doesn't support
People / Organization: Hans van Breukelen (1988 Euro final vs. USSR); Ricardo (2004 vs. Beckham)
Context: Keepers meeting the taker at the spot to unsettle him while he places the ball.
What Happened: Van Breukelen had "a quiet word" while pointing to his own eye, then saved; Ricardo waited
hands-on-hips for Beckham (who ballooned it).
Outcome: Vivid saves — but Jordet's data shows keepers who show up at the spot do NOT induce more misses
than keepers who stay back; for Alves/Martínez it only sets up later, more effective acts.
Concept Illustrated: Anecdote-vs-data gap; a famous tactic with no aggregate effect.
Why This Case Is Useful: Directly supports the chapter's honest twist; a counter-example to intuition.
Potential for Reuse: Medium
```

## Potential Disagreements to Track Later

- **Multiple comparisons / p-hacking sensibility.** The "only significant result" framing of the keeper
  market-value effect is exactly what significance-reform authors (and any Bayesian) would flag as a likely
  false positive. Prime cross-book collision for the book profile's §14 (weaknesses) and §16 (math/decision
  science) — how the book handles significance across the whole work.
- **Sports-media glorification vs. the null data.** The Martínez/Alves legend (and the chapter's own vivid
  storytelling) pulls against Jordet's own mostly-non-significant numbers — a tension between narrative and evidence.
- **Game-theory of keeper positioning** (off-kilter, roaming) links to the Ch.2 minimax tension already flagged.

## Additional Content Opportunities

- **"The Tactic Everyone Copies That Doesn't Work" (Short).** Penalty-spot confrontation + most visual
  distraction show no aggregate effect, yet keeper distraction went from <10% to 50–60% — a
  belief-outrunning-evidence / cargo-cult story. Principle: Signal vs. Noise / survivorship bias.
- **"They Rehearse the Chaos" (Short).** Martínez's "I practised it with my psychologist" reframes mind
  games as a trained skill, not a temperament — pairs with the deliberate-practice theme.
