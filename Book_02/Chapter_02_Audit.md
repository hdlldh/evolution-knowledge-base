## Missing Items

1. **The Rapinoe epigraph (p.51).** The chapter opens with Megan Rapinoe: what has made her successful is
   "the realisation and the acceptance that I will miss them." This is not decoration — it is the
   *acceptance* thesis of the "Mindset" section stated up front, and it connects the pause/mindset material
   to Ch.1's fear-of-failure. The extraction omits it entirely.

2. **Kane's variation switch (p.60).** After ~80 goalkeeper-independent penalties, Kane *switched* to a
   goalkeeper-dependent kick, waiting for the keeper, in the 2023-24 Champions League quarter-final vs.
   Arsenal. The chapter's explicit point — "Variation helps penalty takers keep ahead of the opposition" —
   is a distinct sub-claim (technique is not fixed; elite takers can toggle) that the extraction folds away.
   Lewandowski's mirror-image datum (21 pre-committed kicks, 81%) is captured, but the *variation principle*
   itself is not named.

3. **The two specialists rosters with conversion data (pp.59, 61).** The extraction names some specialists
   but drops most of the exact numbers. Reusable, cross-book-comparable data:
   - Goalkeeper-*independent* specialists: Chris Wood 90.6% (32), Néstor Ortigoza 91.8% (49), Olivier
     Giroud 89.2% (37).
   - Goalkeeper-*dependent* practitioners: Michel Platini 95.7% (46), Raúl Jiménez 94.4% (36), Ivan Toney
     93.8% (32), Sébastien Haller 93.3% (30), Bruno Fernandes 89.8% (59), Erling Haaland 89.4% (47), Mikel
     Oyarzabal 88.9% (36), Gabriel Barbosa 86% (50).

4. **The two-directional risk of timing (p.70).** The extraction captures the *forced-pause* downside (the
   archers) but underweights the chapter's symmetric point that pausing has a Goldilocks shape: those who
   rush (under a second) miss more, *and* those "caught overthinking" who wait too long also suffer. The
   pause is not "longer is better"; it is "own your optimal window."

5. **Minor stature/context details.** Endō as Japan's most-capped player ever (150 caps, 1100+ games,
   retired 2024 at 43) — colour that makes the case more citable. Not essential.

## Overgeneralizations

- **"Down the middle is the best place to shoot" (§9).** The extraction states the 77.9% figure crisply.
  The chapter's implicit condition — the middle only wins *because* keepers usually vacate it, so its edge
  erodes precisely to the extent keepers "mix it up and stay in the middle at least occasionally" (p.57) —
  is present in the extraction's §5 limitation but not carried into the §9 counterintuitive card. Keep the
  condition attached wherever the claim travels.
- **"Predictability is fine" (§9).** Adequately conditioned on Szoboszlai's exceptional strike quality; no
  change needed, but see the game-theory disagreement below — as a *general* claim it is contestable.

## Important Nuance Lost

- The Rapinoe acceptance framing (above) is the biggest nuance gap: the chapter's mindset argument is not
  only "anxiety is normal" but "accept that you *will* fail sometimes," which is a stronger, more specific
  claim than the extraction's §2/§9 phrasing.
- The pause's Goldilocks shape (above).

## Potential Disagreements to Track Later

- **Game theory / minimax (Palacios-Huerta and the mixed-strategy tradition).** Jordet's Szoboszlai point
  — that a *predictable* taker (14/16 to the same side) is fine if the strike is good enough — sits in
  tension with the well-known economics-of-penalties literature arguing that elite takers approximate an
  optimal *mixed* (randomised) strategy, and that exploitable predictability is punished. This is a prime
  cross-book collision for §13/§22 of the book profile: **is penalty success about execution quality or
  about un-exploitable randomisation?** Jordet leans execution; game theorists lean randomisation.
- **"Aim high" (EPL/Primeira Liga studies) vs. "middle scores most" (Jordet's data).** An unresolved
  internal tension already flagged in §11; worth watching against any book that gives a single placement rule.
- **The lab-vs-field methodological stance** (carried from the Introduction): Jordet privileges archival
  real-shootout data over laboratory penalty simulations — a standing disagreement with the experimental
  choking literature that will recur across the book.

## Additional Content Opportunities

- **"Accept That You'll Miss" (Short).** Rapinoe's epigraph + the acceptance mindset (Haaland, Zidane) →
  the counterintuitive claim that planning to sometimes fail is what frees elite performers to succeed.
  Principle: Cognitive Bias / reappraisal. Strong, and currently unbuilt because the epigraph was dropped.
- **"The Goldilocks Pause" (Short/Visual Explainer).** Rushing misses; overthinking-and-waiting misses;

  single U-shaped-curve visual.

   of the opposition."

   goalkeeper-dependent conversion rates) so the numbers are preserved for cross-book use.

   explicitly — rushing *and* over-waiting both hurt; the pause is an owned optimum, not "longer is better."

7. **§11 / cross-book note:** add the **game-theory/minimax disagreement** (execution quality vs.
   un-exploitable randomisation) as a tracked external tension feeding the book profile's §13/§22.
