## Missing Items

1. **Jordet's rebuttal to Blatter — "a penalty kick is a team event" (p.211), omitted.** When Blatter says
   "penalties are not about a team, they are about individuals," Jordet directly disagrees: "I beg to differ:
   a penalty kick is a team event." This is the Epilogue explicitly restating the book's central Ch.4 thesis
   *against FIFA's own president* — a pointed, reusable disagreement the extraction currently drops (it
   captures Blatter's quotes but not the rebuttal). Belongs in the Blatter case and/or §12.

2. **The "just get it over with" escapism phrase (p.210), minor.** The early-decades avoidance is captured in
   the four-eras framework, but the specific "just get it over with" mentality is a direct callback to Ch.1's
   *speeding up* (treating the whistle as a starting gun) — worth one linking phrase.

3. **The "self-proclaimed football intellectuals" framing (p.212), minor colour.** The extraction captures
   the concept (the anti-lottery consensus's blind spot) but not this pointed phrase.

## Potential Disagreements to Track Later

- **The game-theory / minimax tradition is *resolved* here, not merely flagged.** The first-mover-advantage
  decay (61%→49% as teams "neutralise the luck of the draw") arguably *vindicates* the minimax/adaptation view
  (players converge to un-exploitable play) that Ch.2 and Ch.3 were in tension with. This is a key item for
  the book profile's §13/§22: the book ends up partly conceding the economics-of-penalties tradition it
  elsewhere resisted. Already noted in §11, but it should surface prominently in the cross-book synthesis.
- **The self-acknowledged limits of the observational method** (small-n, interpretation-heavy, "findings date
  fast") — a standing caveat the profile's Weaknesses section should carry.
