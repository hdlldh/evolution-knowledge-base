# Algorithms to Live By: The Computer Science of Human Decisions — Conclusion: Computational Kindness
**Author:** Brian Christian and Tom Griffiths
**Type:** Audit
**Source:** sources/Algorithm.to.Live.By.pdf, PDF pages 324–331 (re-read against Chapter_99_Conclusion_Extraction.md)
**Date:** 2026-07-22

Adversarial re-read of the Conclusion against the first-pass extraction. Because the Conclusion is short
and synthetic, the audit is correspondingly focused. Findings grouped by type; "Recommended Changes" are
the subset applied back into the extraction.

---

## 1. Missing Items (present in the text but absent or thin in the extraction)

- **Mall-parking stress → less spending.** The text makes a concrete, non-obvious point: when mall
  parking becomes a source of stress, "shoppers may spend less money and return less frequently" — i.e.,
  computational unkindness has a measurable commercial cost, not just an abstract one. The extraction's
  parking material omits this business hook. Added.

- **"One of the chief goals of design ought to be protecting people from unnecessary tension, friction,
  and mental labor."** A near-thesis sentence for the design half of the Conclusion; the extraction
  captures the idea but not this crisp formulation. Worth a line in the design case.

- **The bus-stop Bayesian callback made explicit.** The extraction mentions "when the last bus left" as a
  proxy (ch. 6), which is correct, but the text ties it specifically to cities that lack live-arrival
  prediction — the callback is to chapter 6's Bayesian prediction, and it's a nice cross-book thread.
  Adequately represented; noting only.

## 2. Corrections Needed (statements that distort the text)

- **The penguin figure.** The extraction's study card reports people "donate more money to save the life
  of one penguin than eight thousand penguins." The text says exactly this — confirmed. But the
  extraction should keep the flag that this (and the terrorism claim) is cited *without* attribution;
  Tension #2 and the study card already do. No change beyond ensuring the flag stays prominent.

- **Shallit's institution and year.** "University of Waterloo," "2003" — both match the text. Confirmed,
  no change.

## 3. Overgeneralizations (author hedges flattened)

- **"Computation is bad."** The extraction quotes this as an "implicit principle of computer science" and
  the authors hedge it as "odd as it may sound." The extraction's Card 3 and Insight #1 preserve the hedge
  ("computation is bad" in quotes, framed as a directive to minimize thought). Adequate — no change.

- **Computational kindness as "ethics."** The authors call it "a surprising bridge from computer science
  to ethics" and "a principle that we call computational kindness" — clearly their own coinage and an
  analogy, not a proven ethical theory. The extraction labels the parent claim "Interpretive (the book's
  original bridge to ethics)" — correctly hedged. Good.

## 4. Important Nuance Lost

- **"We don't only pick the problems we pose to ourselves."** The pivot from the three pieces of wisdom to
  computational kindness turns on this sentence — we also pick the problems we pose *each other* (how we
  design a city, how we ask a question). The extraction's thesis captures the bridge but not this exact
  hinge; minor, and adequately conveyed.

- **Being kinder to ourselves = "more forgiving."** The extraction's Card 8 and §13 capture self-
  compassion, but the text's explicit move — computational kindness turned inward means *self-forgiveness*
  for bounded rationality — is the emotional payoff of the whole book. Retained in spirit; the phrasing
  "more forgiving" is worth keeping visible. Adequate.

## 5. Additional Cases Worth Capturing

- **Mall parking's commercial cost** — see Missing Items; folded into the design case as the concrete
  stakes of computational (un)kindness.

## 6. Additional Research / Scholars Mentioned

- **Jeffrey Shallit (University of Waterloo, 2003)** — named and correctly placed. Retained.
- **Bertrand Russell** (the "wisest act" quote) and **Merrill Flood** (epigraph — the same Flood from ch.
  1 and ch. 8) — both named. Retained. (Flood's recurrence across chapters is a nice cross-book thread for
  the Book Profile.)

## 7. Potential Disagreements / Counterpoints the Chapter Doesn't Raise

- **Constraint vs. autonomy.** Reducing others' options can slide from kindness into control; the
  extraction's Tension #1 raises this. Adequate.

- **Unattributed "celebrated studies."** The penguin/terrorism claims lack sources; per the book's own
  non-invention standard they should be treated as illustration. The extraction's Tension #2 and study
  card flag this. Adequate.

## 8. Additional Content Opportunities

- The mall-parking "spend less, return less" fact strengthens the design video's business case (kindness
  isn't just nice, it's profitable). Folded into the existing §17 design hook rather than added as a new
  block.

## 9. Recommended Changes (applied back into Chapter_99_Conclusion_Extraction.md)

1. **§7 — Add the mall-parking commercial cost** (stress → shoppers spend less and return less
   frequently) to the design case, as the concrete stakes of computational kindness.

2. **§7 / §2 — Add the "protecting people from unnecessary tension, friction, and mental labor"
   formulation** as the design half's near-thesis line (in the design case and the Computational Kindness
   concept).

All other first-pass content is confirmed accurate against the text. The entry is appropriately scaled to
a short Conclusion; the separation of author-claim / evidence / own-inference (esp. §15 sports and §16 AI,
both explicitly inferred) holds up on re-read, as does the flag that the penguin/terrorism examples are
unattributed illustration rather than cited evidence.
