## Missing Items

1. **Explicit disclaimer naming "the Singularity" and "Skynet"** (book p.53): Immediately after discussing Hawkins's theory, the authors write: "We do not speculate on whether this progress heralds the arrival of general artificial intelligence, 'the Singularity,' or Skynet." This is a distinct, more pointed disclaimer than the general "agnostic on prediction vs. intelligence" statement captured in the extraction — it specifically names two well-known AI-risk/AI-hype concepts (the Singularity, and Skynet from *The Terminator*) that the authors decline to engage with, which is useful for cross-referencing against AI-risk-focused books.
2. **The three named industries where churn management is "perhaps the most important marketing activity"** (book p.46): The chapter specifically names insurance, financial services, and telecommunications as service industries where, because customers are expensive to acquire, managing churn is described as "perhaps the most important marketing activity." This concrete detail — which industries, and the superlative framing — was omitted from the extraction, which discusses churn prediction generally without naming these three sectors.
3. **Concrete regression variable examples for churn (cable TV and mobile phone)** (book p.48, p.50): The chapter gives two specific illustrative variables: for cable TV churn, "how frequently people watch TV; if they aren't using their cable subscription, then they are likely to stop subscribing"; and for a mobile phone churn model, researchers used "hour-by-hour call records in addition to standard variables such as bill size and payment punctuality." These concrete, checkable examples were omitted from the extraction's more abstract discussion of churn variables.
