## Missing Items

1. **The specific list of Google's AI-first-era announcements (book p.203)**: The chapter names three concrete examples of what followed Pichai's announcement: "from the development of specialized chips for optimizing machine learning, to the use of deep learning in new applications including cancer research, to putting Google's AI-driven assistant on as many devices as possible." The extraction's Section 3/7 mention the announcement generically without this specific three-part list, which grounds the abstract "AI-first" claim in concrete, checkable examples.
2. **Pichai's specific "searching and organizing" transition quote (book p.204)**: A second, distinct quote from Pichai is missing: he "claimed the company was transitioning from 'searching and organizing the world's information to AI and machine learning.'" This is a separate, quotable articulation of Google's strategic shift beyond the "mobile-first to AI-first" framing already captured, worth its own mention since it specifically names what Google considers its prior core mission (search/organizing information) versus its new one.
3. **The explicit callback to Chapter 17's "Google is Iowa" hybrid corn analogy (book p.205)**: The chapter states directly: "These companies, drawing on the hybrid corn analogy from chapter 17, are like the farmers located in Iowa." The extraction's Concept 4 (innovator's dilemma applied to AI) discusses the general dynamic of incumbents/margins/experimentation capacity but does not capture this specific, deliberate textual cross-reference the book makes back to its own earlier Ch.17 metaphor — a genuine missed connection between chapters that reinforces the book's internal continuity.
4. **The "training manual, not user manual" quotable line (book p.206)**: A sharp, memorable line is missing from the extraction: "You won't need a user manual so much as a training manual. This training requires some way for the AI to gather data and improve." This directly follows the claim that off-the-shelf AI solutions are rare when AI is core to a business's own purpose, and deserves inclusion as a standalone quotable idea given its concision and memorability.
5. **The specific "wait-and-see" temptation and catch-up-difficulty risk from Key Points (book p.218)**: The chapter's Key Points state a more specific strategic risk than the extraction's general innovator's-dilemma framing conveys: "It is tempting for established companies to take a wait-and-see approach, standing on the sidelines and observing the progress in AI applied to their industry. That may work for some companies, but others will find it difficult to catch up once their competitors get ahead in the training and deployment of their AI tools." The extraction's Concept 4 captures the general disruption dynamic but not this specific, actionable warning about the wait-and-see temptation and the asymmetric catch-up risk it creates.
