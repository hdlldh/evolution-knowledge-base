## Missing Items

1. **Toronto's outsized influence on industrial AI teams** (book p.2): The chapter states that experts previously based in the University of Toronto computer science department "today head several of the world's leading industrial AI teams, including those at Meta (Facebook), Apple, and OpenAI." This concrete, name-droppable claim was omitted from the extraction. It strengthens the "why trust these authors" case and is independently a strong content hook (Toronto as an AI origin hub).
2. **The book's four-audience value proposition** (book p.3): The chapter explicitly lays out four distinct reader personas and what the book offers each: business leaders (understanding of AI's impact on management/decisions), students/recent graduates (a framework for thinking about the evolution of jobs and careers), financial analysts/VCs (a structure for investment theses), and policymakers (guidelines for how AI might change society and how policy should respond). This structural detail was absent from the extraction entirely — it is useful both as a map of the book's intended uses and as a template for a "who is this for" content asset.
3. **"To our knowledge" hedge on the CDL superlative claim** (book p.2): The claim that CDL "had, over eight years, the greatest concentration of AI startups of any program on earth" is explicitly hedged with "To our knowledge, as of September 2022." The extraction's Section 7 case entry paraphrases the claim but drops both the hedge and the specific date, which matters — this is an unverifiable, author-asserted superlative, not an external fact.
4. **Decisions under incomplete information** (book p.4): "You will need to make decisions without full information, but doing so will often be better than inaction." This is a distinct claim about the value of acting under uncertainty, separate from the trade-offs claim already captured, and was omitted.
5. **The book's self-aware durability claim** (book p.4): "The examples we use will surely become dated, the framework in this book will not" — an explicit claim about why a framework-first approach was chosen over an examples-first approach. Relevant to how the book intends to be read/used over time; omitted from extraction.

## Potential Disagreements to Track Later

- The "AI is just prediction, not intelligence" framing (already flagged in the extraction's Section 11) is a stance likely to be contested by AI researchers and cognitive scientists who would argue that modern AI systems (especially post-2022 large language models, given this book's 2022 "Updated and Expanded" edition) exhibit capabilities — reasoning, generation, planning — that don't reduce cleanly to "prediction" in the narrow sense used here. Worth tracking against any AI/ML-focused book in the knowledge base for a direct comparison point.
- The book's own preface (read during intake, not part of Chapter 1 proper) already flags a related self-revision: the authors later distinguish "prediction" from "judgment" as two decoupled inputs to decision-making, and wrote a follow-up book (*Power & Prediction*) specifically because this decoupling turned out to be more consequential than the original book emphasized. This is worth tracking as an internal tension between the original (2018) and updated (2022) framing — flagged here for the book profile's "argument that shifts between early and late material" check, though it's visible only in the Preface, not Chapter 1 itself.
