## Missing Items

1. **The chapter's explicit statement of the WWII case's argumentative purpose** (book p.36): Immediately after the D-Day/weather-station narrative, the authors write: "The point of this story is not to support the rather obvious claim that predictions are valuable. Instead, it is to demonstrate precisely *when* they are valuable. Predictions can turn an otherwise risky option into a less risky one." This is a meta-level methodological statement — the authors are explicit that the case isn't meant as generic "prediction is good" evidence but as a demonstration of the specific mechanism (converting risky, time-bound, irreversible decisions into manageable ones). The extraction's case entry captures the story and a general "concept illustrated" but doesn't capture the authors' own explicit framing of *why* they chose this example over a simpler one.
2. **Modern maritime insurance's evolution into "exotic" products like ransom insurance** (book p.33): The chapter notes that maritime insurance survives today, is "likely considerably cheaper as shipping became safer," but also "somewhat more exotic as new products such as ransom insurance (in case your ship is taken by pirates) emerged and became predictable enough to be priced." This concrete modern detail — showing insurance markets evolving to price newly-quantifiable risks — was omitted from the Lloyd's case entry, which stops at the historical origin story.

## Important Nuance Lost

- The chapter frames the entire "Predictions Mitigate Risk" section around a specific rhetorical move: business leaders don't need convincing that weather forecasts are valuable for daily decisions (umbrella, coat), so the authors deliberately escalate to a *high-stakes, low-margin-for-error* domain (wartime) to make the more precise point about *when and how* predictions matter, not just that they do. The extraction's Section 1 thesis captures the "when" framing generally but doesn't flag that the authors chose this escalation (daily → wartime) deliberately as a rhetorical strategy.
