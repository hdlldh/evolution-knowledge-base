## Missing Items

1. **Uncertainty reduction hits both the benefit and cost sides of the dilemma simultaneously (book p.181)**: The chapter states this as a distinct, non-obvious point: "If you can accurately forecast what people will purchase, especially if delivered to their doorsteps, then you reduce the likelihood of returns and increase sales. Uncertainty reduction hits both the benefit and the cost sides of the dilemma." The extraction's Key Claims (Amazon case) captures both effects (increased sales, reduced returns) but doesn't explicitly flag the chapter's own framing that a *single* uncertainty-reducing capability moves *both* sides of the trade-off at once — a sharper, more general point about why AI-driven uncertainty reduction can be unusually decisive in tipping strategic trade-offs, worth stating as its own claim.
2. **The "dependent on others for special seeds" parallel in the hybrid corn story (book p.182)**: The chapter notes hybrid corn "was a critical change because not only did it promise dramatically higher yields, but the farmer became dependent on others for special seeds." The extraction's hybrid corn concept/case captures the adoption-timing/ROI analogy but omits this specific dependency parallel — a direct analogy to businesses becoming dependent on AI vendors/providers for a capability they can no longer produce in-house, which sharpens the corn analogy's relevance to AI strategy beyond just adoption speed.
3. **The "land"/"varieties" farming metaphor is extended into the Google/AI discussion (book p.184)**: The chapter continues its corn metaphor explicitly: enormous corporations "can take a part of the 'land' and devote it to many new AI varieties." The extraction's Section 3 (Google claim) and Section 7 (hybrid corn case) paraphrase this as generic "afford to experiment broadly" language, losing the chapter's own deliberate literary device of carrying the corn-farming metaphor forward into the AI-strategy discussion.
4. **Scouts retained a role in player selection even after prediction machines were introduced (book p.185)**: The chapter states directly: "the scouts continued to have a role in player selection." The extraction's Moneyball case (Section 7) captures that on-field play barely changed but omits this specific point that human scouts weren't eliminated either — reinforcing the augmentation-not-elimination pattern established in Ch.16, now applied specifically to the pre-sabermetrics judgment role (scouts) rather than only to the new roles created.
5. **"Analytics experts have mathematical skills, but the finest of them... provide judgment" (book p.185)**: A specific, quotable closing claim from the baseball section is missing from the extraction: "These analytics experts have mathematical skills, but the finest of them understand best what to tell the prediction machine to do. They provide judgment." This is a sharp distinction the extraction's broader "prediction's complements gain value" concept doesn't state as precisely — that raw quantitative skill alone doesn't define the most valuable new hires; judgment about what to tell the machine to optimize does.
6. **The "nerds" / "jock world" characterization and the requirement that new analytics hires still need deep game understanding (book p.186)**: The chapter's colorful framing is missing: "Teams are increasingly bringing in new senior advisers who sometimes may not have firsthand experience playing the game and—true to stereotype—may not be an obvious fit in the jock world of professional sports. However, even nerds recruited into this setting require a deep understanding of the game..." This adds a distinct point beyond "judgment matters" — that the specific *kind* of judgment required is deep domain (game) understanding, not just general analytical judgment, a nuance the extraction's Key Concept 5 doesn't capture.
7. **An apparent internal inconsistency in the book between body text and Key Points regarding the Amazon returns-truck frequency (book p.181 vs. p.190)**: The main text states Amazon "might establish an infrastructure of trucks that visit neighborhoods **daily** for deliveries and returns" (p.181, stated twice as "daily"), but the chapter's own Key Points summary describes "a fleet of trucks that did **weekly** pickups throughout the neighborhood" (p.190). The extraction followed the body text's "daily" framing without noting this discrepancy with the chapter's own summary — worth flagging as a genuine textual inconsistency in the source rather than silently resolving it one way.
8. **Two distinct, non-identical forward chapter references regarding data value are conflated (book p.187 vs. p.189)**: On p.187, the chapter states "we will explore the advantages of generating new data in the **next chapter**" (i.e., Chapter 18, "When AI Transforms Your Business"). Separately, the Key Points (p.189) state that a prediction machine's data "become so important that you may need to change strategy... as we will show in **chapter 19**." The extraction's Section 19 summary and elsewhere only cite "chapter 19," but the book actually makes two separate forward references to two different future chapters on related-but-distinct data topics — this distinction should be preserved rather than collapsed into a single citation.
