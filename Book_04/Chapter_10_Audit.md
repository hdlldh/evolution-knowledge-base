## Missing Items

1. **The concrete list of strategic "never done it before" business decisions** (book p.113): The chapter gives a specific, named list of strategic business decisions that share the rare-event/no-relevant-data problem: "Should you add a new product to a product line? Should you merge with a competitor? Should you acquire an innovative startup or a channel partner?" This immediately follows the general "What will happen if I do this?" framing and directly precedes the conclusion that "if people will behave differently after the change, then past behavior is not a useful guide for future behavior. The prediction machine will not have relevant data." The extraction's Section 2 (rare events) and Section 10/19 summarized this point abstractly (strategic decisions about new technology) but omitted this specific, concrete list of three business-decision types (product line extension, competitor merger, startup/channel-partner acquisition), which are more commonplace and relatable than the "responding to a new technology" framing already captured.
2. **The specific mechanism "if people will behave differently after the change"** (book p.113): The chapter's precise reasoning for why prediction machines fail on these strategic decisions is that *behavior itself changes* as a result of the decision being contemplated — not simply that the event is rare, but that the change alters the very behavior patterns the historical data was capturing. This is a subtly different and sharper mechanism (related to the "Lucas critique" concept in economics, though the chapter doesn't use that term) than simple data scarcity, and was not captured explicitly in the extraction.
